# Presenter Guidelines — What Level to Pitch a Talk At

Louis's standing answer (2026-08-13) when a speaker asks "what level should this be at?"

## The philosophy

> The goal is to show historians what is possible. The conversation should be at a relatively technical level so that people who know their stuff can get insight, and everybody else can use an AI to look up terms.

Don't pitch to the person in the room who knows the least. Pitch to a sharp colleague. If a term needs defining for a general audience, that's what everyone's own AI assistant is for, mid-talk, silently, without the speaker having to stop and explain it. Use real vocabulary. Anyone can look up "headless scraper," nobody in the room needs to be protected from a term they can query in five seconds.

## Ready-to-send reply

Use or adapt when a speaker asks about pitch/level:

> The audience ranges from people building their own pipelines to people who've never written a line of code, but everyone has an AI assistant on hand. So don't pitch to the least technical person in the room, pitch to a sharp colleague. If you use a term like "headless scraper" or "entity resolution," you don't need to stop and define it. Whoever already knows gets real insight, and everyone else can just ask their own AI what it means on the spot. Use real vocabulary, that's more useful here than a talk paced for the person who knows the least.

## Why this matters for the program

Session descriptions on the site should reflect this too — favor concrete method/tool names and real specifics (benchmarks, error rates, dataset sizes) over vague, softened framing. When writing or revising a session description, don't sand off technical vocabulary in the name of accessibility.
