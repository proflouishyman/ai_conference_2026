Session 26 · Friday, October 16, 2026

Experiments in AI and Handwriting Recognition

Haley Price (Brown University), Anya Lewis-Meeks (Brown University), Ashley Champagne (Brown University)

Friday, October 16, 2026, 1:30 to 2:30 Eastern, Room 107

Watch on YouTube

Abstract

A Brown University team asks whether AI (artificial intelligence) handwriting recognition is solved, running handwriting tools such as Transkribus and then frontier and open-weight models on three collections: excavation notebooks from Abydos in Egypt, Wilson Harris's manuscript notebooks, and the 17th-century letters of Maria Madelena von Habsburg in Florence. The models struggled with locus numbers and with Harris's overwritten drafts, and on 87 hand-verified pages of Habsburg letters Claude Fable did best while Opus 5 fell from first to last under diplomatic transcription rules because it hallucinated. Price explains why a low word error rate can still hide errors that change meaning, such as gender agreement in Italian. The Q&A covers attributing marginalia to John Major and the ethics of giving apprenticeship work to a chatbot.
