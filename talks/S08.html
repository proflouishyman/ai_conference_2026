Who Gets to Argue with the Data? Rent Control and Democratic Knowledge

Jo Guldi (Emory University)

Thursday, October 15, 2026, 1:30 to 2:30 Eastern, Room 242

Watch on YouTube

Abstract

Jo Guldi previews her book The War Over Rent, which applies data methods to a century-long struggle over rent control, beginning in Ireland in 1881, where land valuators such as John Murray gathered evidence for the new Land Court. She argues that bureaucracy can work as repair and that public data helped end a century of conflict, which complicates a Foucauldian suspicion of quantification. She then explains the data work behind the book: counting debates in Hansard (the record of British parliamentary debate) from 1806 to 1911, then using topic modeling and similarity search to find the land valuators and Patrick Sheridan. Searches of prison and newspaper records with large language models (LLMs) supplied character detail, and she calls the overall approach the tapestry method. The talk asks which numbers count and who produces them, and what it takes to keep claims inspectable and contestable when AI systems mediate our encounters with evidence.
