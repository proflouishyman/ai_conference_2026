Trust and Error: AI Hallucination, Bias, and Evidentiary Standards in Historical Research

Edward J. K. Gitre (Virginia Tech), Samuel Backer (University of Maine)

Thursday, October 15, 2026, 1:30 to 2:30 Eastern, Room 108

Watch on YouTube

Abstract

Edward J. K. Gitre and Samuel Backer each present a case of error in computational history and then take audience questions. Gitre describes The American Soldier in World War II, a project that reconciled about 250,000 transcription contributions from roughly 7,600 Zooniverse volunteers using a Jaccard dissimilarity method and hand review, and that found natural language processing (NLP) tools misreading race-related vocabulary. Backer argues that error has to be understood before a method is chosen, using optical character recognition (OCR) and Gensim topic modeling on the Lester S. Levy sheet music collection. He also reports work with Louis Hyman showing that historians need precision more than recall, and a failed attempt to extract ledger data from theater booking records with a large language model (LLM).
