Working with Computer Scientists

Anjalie Field (Johns Hopkins University, Data Science and AI Institute), Daphne Ippolito (Carnegie Mellon University), Michael Harrower (Johns Hopkins University, Near Eastern Studies)

Thursday, October 15, 2026, 3:30 to 4:30 Eastern, Room 108

Watch on YouTube

Abstract

A conversation on how historians can approach and collaborate with computer scientists: why computer scientists find historical material interesting, why they are often confused by historians' methods and questions, and why historians are interesting to them in turn. Field describes a project with sociologist Steve Morgan on five years of Baltimore police radio, where automatic speech recognition (ASR) fails on police terms. Harrower describes automated detection of archaeological sites in satellite imagery, with Bronze Age tombs of the Arabian Peninsula as the example, and a project with Carnegie Mellon University on archaeological databases. Ippolito explains that AI researchers want verifiable rewards and advises first testing whether Claude already solves the problem. A long audience Q&A follows.
