Migrant Connections: Building Digital Infrastructure for Migration History

Atiba Pertilla (German Historical Institute Washington)

Friday, October 16, 2026, 1:30 to 2:30 Eastern, Room 108

Watch on YouTube

Abstract

Atiba Pertilla describes how the German Historical Institute Washington built Migrant Connections, a collection of German immigrant letters that volunteers transcribe on Omeka Classic with the Scripto interface, and how the spinoff project Let the Past Speak combines text-to-speech (TTS) and retrieval-based voice conversion (RVC) to reconstruct 19th century northern Hessian German and Texas German. He then shows how Google Document AI and Claude helped him extract and restructure Baltimore city directory data, including house number changes after the 1887 renumbering, for his study of immigrant financial practices. He argues that AI favors the historian at a workbench over the large digital humanities lab, and the audience discusses voice ethics and errors in archival metadata.
