MapReader: Historical Maps as Data

Katie McDonough (Carnegie Mellon University)

Friday, October 16, 2026, 1:30 to 2:30 Eastern, Room 242

Watch on YouTube

Abstract

Katie McDonough introduces MapReader, an open source library that treats historical maps as data. A map sheet is divided into patches, an image classifier labels each patch, and a text-spotting step reads the words on the map. She draws on the Living with Machines project, where railway patches from Ordnance Survey sheets were linked to the 1901 census, and on the Machines Reading Maps project, where text from the David Rumsey collection was clustered and explored through random walks over a network of words. She opens with principles on open source tools and community maintenance of research software and closes with open problems in multimodal queries with vision language models (VLMs) and in storing map text in library systems.
