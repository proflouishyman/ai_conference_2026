Environmental History and AI

Alexander More (University of Massachusetts Boston and Harvard University)

Friday, October 16, 2026, 11:30 to 12:30 Eastern, Room 112

Watch on YouTube

Abstract

More argues that scientists rather than historians now set the public narrative on climate change, including the pre-industrial baselines behind the 1.5 degree threshold. He presents a geodatabase of about 55,000 geolocated climate records for Eurasia, continuous from 1000 to 1820 and built over ten years by 25 scholars. He shows how AI helped with quality checking by restoring a missing sub-database and splitting reports into one record per phenomenon, then tests the records against tree rings and the North Atlantic Oscillation, finding correlations near 0.5 between 1000 and 1300. The discussion turns to plain language for several audiences, telling gradual change as a story, and extending the data to more regions.
