Abstract

Three speakers consider what AI applied to digitized collections demands of archivists and historians. Richard Marciano introduces computational archival science, a research and teaching program at the University of Maryland, and an earlier computer-vision project on the Morgenthau Diaries. Linde Brocato explains how the Franklin D. Roosevelt (FDR) White House filed and indexed its correspondence, and Rajesh Gnanasekaran presents a proposed six-pass agentic workflow, ending in a Neo4j knowledge graph, for the FDR Presidential Library's gift correspondence. The Q&A turns to provenance and handwriting recognition.
