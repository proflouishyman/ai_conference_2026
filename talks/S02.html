AI and the Misrepresentation of History

Marnie Hughes-Warrington (University of Adelaide)

Thursday, October 15, 2026, 9:30 to 10:30 Eastern, Main Hall

Watch on YouTube

Abstract

Hughes-Warrington argues that algorithms which generate histories, which she calls artificial historians, shape access to education, insurance, finance, and justice, so that algorithmic unfairness is a historical problem. She distinguishes person-on-person history wars from algorithmic harms that arise without human intent, with a hybrid phase between them, and shows how multi-agent systems and safety layers in artificial intelligence (AI) models can concentrate attention or distort the record. She draws on economics work on algorithmic collusion and on a misinformation study to propose a research agenda on differential historical attention in training data and on agent timing, and asks how models perform in other languages. Her book Artificial Historians (Routledge, 2025) is open access.
