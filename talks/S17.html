The Discipline We Need to Build

Christopher Phillips (Carnegie Mellon University), Matthew Connelly (Columbia University), Jo Guldi (Emory University), Sarah Weicksel (American Historical Association (AHA))

Friday, October 16, 2026, 9:30 to 10:30 Eastern, Main Hall

Abstract

A plenary panel, moderated by Sarah Weicksel of the American Historical Association (AHA), on which institutional changes, from graduate training to funding, would let computational history become a durable part of the discipline rather than a permanent margin. Matthew Connelly points to the post-2008 collapse of the job market and the risk to junior scholars who spend years cleaning data, Jo Guldi describes the lost promise of digital history because funding and hiring never followed, and Christopher Phillips says the long timescale of historical work and its focus on specific evidence slow change. The panel then argues for collectively chosen ambitious goals, including Guldi's proposed global index of climate harms, and Connelly compares the roughly 48 billion dollar annual budget of the National Institutes of Health (NIH) with the roughly 400 million dollar budget of the National Archives in making the case for a National Institute for History. Loren Moulds presents Discord poll results from attendees, and the audience asks how to present AI-assisted methods on the job market.
