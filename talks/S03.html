Reading the Machine: What Large Language Models Actually Do to Historical Text

Mark Humphries (Wilfrid Laurier University)

Thursday, October 15, 2026, 11:30 to 12:30 Eastern, Online

Watch on YouTube

Abstract

Humphries walks through how large language models (LLMs) handle historical language and handwriting at scale, drawing on his record-linkage work with 10 million pages of digitized First World War veterans' pension files and an ongoing fur-trade project with Lianne Leddy. He argues that models such as Gemini now show something like abstract reasoning, citing its transcription of an unpublished Albany ledger, and reports a word error rate of about 1.8 percent on 18th and 19th century English handwriting. He demonstrates the Archive Pearl site for transcription, segmentation, metadata extraction and map pin placement, and warns that models are least reliable at reporting negatives. He urges historians to benchmark models on tasks with known answers and to build verification into every stage.
